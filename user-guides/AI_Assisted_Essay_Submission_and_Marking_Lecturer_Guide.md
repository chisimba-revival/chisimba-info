# AI-assisted Essay submission and marking: lecturer guide

## What Essays now provides

**Essays** is the single course entry point for long-form written assessment. Students may write in Chisimba's rich-text editor or upload a completed document. Every Essay is marked out of 100. Its contribution to the final course mark belongs in Gradebook's **Assessment Plan** and **Assessment Sheet**, not in the Essay setup form.

AI can prepare a suggested percentage and feedback from the submitted text, essay brief and attached rubric. It never publishes a mark by itself. A lecturer must review and save an individual draft or deliberately use **Approve all** for the completed drafts in a topic.

## Prepare an Essay activity

1. Open **Essays** in the course. Lecturers arrive in the Essay management workspace.
2. Add a topic area with a clear description, instructions and closing date.
3. Attach a suitable rubric when one is available.
4. Add one or more Essay titles. Use the notes to state requirements such as expected length, evidence, structure and referencing.
5. If the result belongs in Gradebook, open **Grade Book → Assessment Plan** and add the Essay topic.
6. Open the **Assessment Sheet** and allocate its contribution to the final course mark. A 0% contribution displays the result without affecting the course total.

## Student submission choices

After booking an Essay, a student opens **My essays** and chooses **Write or upload essay**.

### Write online

The student writes in the TinyMCE editor. **Save draft** preserves the work without making it available for marking. **Submit for marking** finalises the current editor text; the student cannot continue editing that submission afterwards.

### Upload a document

The student selects an existing file and chooses **Upload and submit**. This submits immediately. Chisimba safely extracts text from supported plain-text, DOCX and ODT documents for AI review. Unsupported file types remain available for ordinary lecturer download and manual marking; Chisimba does not invent AI feedback from a filename.

## Mark one Essay manually

1. Open **Essays**, find the topic and choose **Mark essays**.
2. Open the submitted Essay.
3. Read the student's online text or download the submitted document.
4. Review the rubric when one is attached.
5. Enter a whole-number mark from 0 to 100 and constructive feedback.
6. Optionally attach a returned marked document.
7. Choose **Save**.

The lecturer's saved mark and feedback are authoritative. Students can see them in **My essays**, and Gradebook reads the percentage through the shared assessment-provider interface.

## Prepare and review one AI draft

Open a submitted Essay and choose **Suggest mark and feedback with AI**. Chisimba queues the work; the browser may be closed while the shared worker processes it. When ready, the page shows the suggested percentage and feedback in editable fields, specific strengths, suggested improvements and matters that require careful human judgement.

Read the submission and rubric, correct the draft as necessary, then choose **Approve AI suggestion** or **Save**. Both use the same validated lecturer-save path. A mark outside 0–100 is rejected, and the transient AI draft is removed after the final mark is saved.

## Prepare and approve a topic in bulk

On the topic's **Mark essays** page, choose **Prepare AI drafts for all**. Only eligible, submitted, text-readable and currently unmarked Essays are queued. Existing queued, running or completed drafts are not duplicated.

When processing finishes, each row shows **AI draft ready**. You may open individual rows to inspect and edit them. **Approve all** then saves every completed draft shown for that topic as its final mark and feedback. It does not approve failed, pending, unreadable or manually marked submissions.

Use **Approve all** only after the cohort-level review required by your institution. The action is a lecturer decision, not automatic marking.

## If AI is unavailable

Manual marking always remains available. A queued request may be refreshed later; a failed request does not change a student's mark. If work remains queued for several minutes, give the administrator the course, Essay topic and approximate queue time so they can check the shared workers.

## Lecturer checklist

- Clear brief and requirements
- Relevant rubric attached where useful
- Essay topic added to the Assessment Plan
- Contribution allocated in the Assessment Sheet
- Editor and upload routes considered for student access
- AI drafts checked against the actual submission
- Review flags resolved by a person
- Individual or bulk approval deliberately completed
- Learner feedback and Gradebook percentage checked
